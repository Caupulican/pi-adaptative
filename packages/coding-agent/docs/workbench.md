# Interactive Workbench

Human interactive sessions use a full-screen Workbench with the selected terminal theme, including Matrix. Unattended sessions retain their transcript layout.

Rows, top to bottom: a title strip (application name, the plan or goal naming the work or else the session name, and the working directory with its branch right-aligned — identity and location only), the work area (Work plan and Team on the left, Execution on the right), its divider (a drag handle, `↕`, while expanded; the blocks' summary while collapsed), the conversation zone (the **Decision graph** on the left and the conversation on the right, or the conversation alone when the graph is hidden or the width cannot hold both), the **live row** (the one run-state indicator: `● subject (elapsed)`; the subject is the single running tool or worker when there is exactly one, otherwise the live runtime label such as *Thinking* or *Working*, with the plan step, concurrency counts, the message queue and the newest event on the right; the row is reserved and blank while idle so geometry never jumps), the **operator POV bar** (see below) on the status band's surface, the status band (transient notices, extension widgets and session status), the editor inside its own top and bottom rules, extension widgets below it, and one key-hint row that names every key. Panes that sit side by side are separated by a one-column dim rule; where a rule meets the divider the junction (`┴`, `┬`, `┼`) is composed into the divider. Zones separate by surface tone (`workbenchSurface`, optional in custom themes and defaulting to the custom-message background), spacing and those rules; nothing is boxed.

The default layout stays stacked: work area above, conversation below. `Alt+P` puts Conversation on the left and Execution on the right of the same rows (drag the gutter to resize; the conversation header's **Stacked** chip or `Alt+P` restores the default). **Hide** still removes the work plan so code takes the whole right column. The inspector on the left (30% of the width by default: a **Work plan** block with completed/total steps and a **Team** block with active agents, each with a quiet placeholder when empty) and **Execution** on the right (file effects and command outcomes, with a placeholder until the first evidence) remain the work-area contents. By default it takes half of the rows left after the title strip, the live row and the status band (so a tall terminal gives evidence and conversation the same room); the conversation always keeps at least six rows, and only the operator changes the geometry. The first `Alt+=` / `Alt+-` turns the split into an explicit row count that persists. `Alt+O` (or, with the mouse captured, a click on the divider) collapses it to the divider row, which then summarizes the blocks, and expands it again; dragging that divider resizes the work area (a drag onto the title strip collapses it). `Alt+=` and `Alt+-` add or remove a row; `Alt+I` or **Hide** on the Work plan title hides the inspector so Execution takes the full width, and **Show plan** on the Execution title shows it again; `Alt+X` or **Maximize** on the Execution title maximizes Execution to every row the conversation minimum leaves (the inspector hidden meanwhile) and **Restore** returns the previous rows. Dragging the gap between the panes changes the inspector's width (20%–45%). Rows, collapsed state, inspector visibility, inspector width, layout (`stacked` or `columns`), conversation column width, the maximized flag, the preview retention and the Decision graph's visibility, width and view persist in settings (`workbench.rows`, `workbench.collapsed`, `workbench.inspector`, `workbench.inspectorFraction`, `workbench.layout`, `workbench.conversationFraction`, `workbench.executionMaximized`, `workbench.previews`, `workbench.graph`, `workbench.graphFraction`, `workbench.graphView`) and are restored at start; each pane title carries one chip (**Hide** on the Work plan, **Maximize** / **Restore** or **Show plan** on Execution), every other action lives on the hint row's keys. Tool output never moves the conversation boundary: previews scroll inside their surface. Terminals narrower than 80 columns stack Execution above the inspector, and very short terminals show both in one scrolling surface or, below two rows, only the divider. Status and input stay anchored below the conversation. Extremely small terminals omit gutters to preserve native input and its cursor. A question dialog sits in the editor slot and receives a row budget from the host, so a question with many options scrolls inside its lane (the active option, the title and the help row stay visible) instead of taking over the screen.

Evidence stays on screen until it is replaced: completion does not fold it, and a new cycle keeps the previous cycle's previews and counters until its own first tool result, file effect or Jev evaluation arrives. Every preview title says who produced it: a foreground tool receipt carries `root · <model>` (the model actually answering when the receipt was created, so a routed turn is attributed to the routed model), a workspace observation carries `root` or `root + N workers` with no model claimed, and a settled Jev evaluation is its own preview on the cyan tone — `◆ System One <label>` with `system one · <verdict> · <duration>` on the right and its reasons as the body; a verdict noted later replaces that preview in place. System One previews count toward the preview retention but never toward the tool accounting. Failure counts remain visible until session replacement. A finished plan folds to one `Work complete` row and an idle team to one `Team idle · N sessions retained` row without deleting tasks, worker sessions or durable evidence. A retired worker (`delegate retire`) is no longer a retained session: it leaves the Team row while its binding and transcript stay reachable through `delegate list` / `transcript`. Long plans show up to six prioritized rows (blocked and failed first, then active, then pending) with the hidden count; use the existing work/team inspector for the complete state. Edit previews carry their added/removed line counts.

The **Team** block is a hierarchy read from live state on every frame. **Decider** comes first: `◆ System One` with what it is judging and a running clock while an evaluation is in flight, or `◇` with its last verdict and label, or `ready` / `off`; beneath it who holds control (`decides next` while CONTROL is S1, `waiting for you` while a question is open, `root executes` otherwise). **Executors** follow: the root first (`●` while it is running, with its active model and current action) and then the lanes as before, specialists before workers. **Routing** appears only while a routed choice is live: the router's choice for the root (`<tier> via model-router → <model> for root`) and each running worker's pinned profile (`profile <id> → <model> for <label>`); nothing is credited to the router that it did not choose.

## Decision graph

The pane left of the conversation is System One's live dashboard: it shows what the loop needed, what it did, where it is and what comes next, composed from canonical state on every frame — the operator projection and its **stage log**, the Jev evaluation ledger, the router's foreground snapshot, lane records, the task's steps and the goal's requirements, verification obligations, the cycle's receipts, the lane's background tools and the questions asked. It is a projection, not a second state machine, and it holds no timer of its own: the lane's one-second ticker also covers the graph while it carries a running clock.

The stage log derives one of ten stages from each published projection — `understand`, `plan`, `build`, `dispatch` (a non-root actor executing), `observe` (System One reviewing evidence), `verify`, `clarify` (a question to you), `repair` (a repair-class reason code or a block), `deliver`, `done` — and records every transition with its time, so a stage that runs twice keeps the accumulated time of its closed visits, while the ticking clock is only the current visit and starts again when that stage is entered. Entering `repair` increments the loop number. An idle session is not a stage: with no objective and no turn running, the log opens nothing, the graph reads "No task yet" and no clock runs; a turn without a goal is recorded as one `build` pass by the root, closed when the turn ends; a paused, limited or cancelled objective is idle the same way. Rows the placeholder projection wrote before this rule (`understand` with `no_objective`) stay in the ledger and are left out when it is read. Transitions are written to the agent directory's decision ledger (`state/decision-ledger.sqlite`, keyed by session id and working directory, append-only: rows settle, they are never deleted), Closed visits keep their durations across a restart. An open visit does not: the next read restarts that clock, so time the process was down is not counted as work. Sessions opened in the same directory never share a row. Every Jev evaluation the session runs — the recording engine, the steering plane's checkpoints and System One's stage validations — starts and settles in the same ledger with its kind, consequence, model, outcome, verdict and bounded reasons.

Two views, switched with the **List** / **Diagram** chips or `Alt+L` and persisted (`workbench.graphView`, `diagram` by default):

- **List**: only the stages that occurred, in the order they were first entered. A closed stage shows its accumulated time and pass count. The current stage shows this visit's clock, which starts over when the stage is entered again, and names the loop (`build · loop 2`, `← now`); then the participants with their models, the routed choices, the evidence and check counts, the judge's last verdict and the `next →` action. A click on a stage (or a click on its node in the Diagram) opens its detail — the note, the reason code, the operator events recorded while the stage was open (at most six), and the evaluation in flight — and a second click closes it.
- **Diagram**: a drawing composed per task, never a fixed skeleton. A `YOU` node with its edge exists only once a question was asked (lit while you are the control owner; the loop row carries the clock); the `SYSTEM ONE` box says what the decider is doing (and carries the in-flight evaluation label; a second row is only the last settled verdict); the plan tree lists the task's steps with their status; one node per participant that exists — the root on its active model, each specialist and worker with its route, a capability being synthesized, a background tool — laid out as siblings with `┌─┬─┐` / `└─┴─┘` connectors computed from their count, or stacked on the spine when the pane is too narrow for them side by side; the `EVIDENCE` and `CHECKS` nodes; the work loop lists every stage that occurred, titled `loop N` or `↺ loop N` after a repair, and the current stage carries the only running clock (this visit, not the lifetime sum); the `goal satisfied?` line is a single `not closed` stem, or `not closed · N open` while checks remain, until a repair or a block makes a real yes/no fork. That fork lights `DELIVER` / `delivered` only when no checks remain open, and otherwise shows `repair` with `↺ loop N → SYSTEM ONE`; and the pending `next →` edge. Visited nodes are lit, unvisited ones dim. The loop is its own row, so a narrow column cannot drop it to keep a clock. Labels shorten before edges break, and the drawing re-lays itself out to the pane's width on every frame.

`Alt+G` or the **Hide** chip hides the pane (`workbench.graph`); the graph folds by itself, keeping the setting, when the conversation zone is narrower than 80 columns or cannot give the graph 48 columns beside a 40-column conversation. With the mouse captured the wheel scrolls the pane (it re-centres on the current step only when that step changes, so your scroll holds), and dragging the rule between the graph and the conversation resizes it (25%–50% of the zone, `workbench.graphFraction`; a drag that returns to its origin restores the previous width and nothing persists until release).

## Operator POV bar

One `|`-separated row on the status band's lane, directly above the editor's top rule, is the only normal-mode operator status surface (the working directory moved to the title strip). Every value is a live read of canonical state on each render — the session's operator projection, the router's foreground route snapshot, the semantic plane's observed health and the session cost summary — never a literal:

```text
WORKING build: Simplifying settings | CONTROL S1 | NEXT verify 5 open criteria | ACTOR worker settings | ROOT gpt-5.6 | ACTIVE claude-sonnet-4-6 | ROUTE medium via model-router/H-MoE | S1 ok | COST $0.083 | PROOF 0/5 | CTX 11.6%
```

- **WORKING** (or **READY** / **BLOCKED** / **DONE**): the canonical phase and current action; `READY` while the session is idle (no objective, or a paused, limited or cancelled one, and no turn running); a block shows its reason.
- **CONTROL**: who owns the next objective transition — `S1` while System One (the goal loop and its semantic plane) owns an active objective, `ROOT` when no objective is active (the root loop decides), `USER` only while an owner question is pending. It is a reason-coded runtime fact derived from the goal continuation verdict and the durable human-input record, never a narration, and it is independent of ACTOR: a worker can be executing (`ACTOR worker …`) while `CONTROL S1` reviews what comes next, and a returned worker is evidence for System One to review (`WORKING build: Reviewing worker result: …`), never completion by itself. The router and H-MoE choose models; they are never CONTROL owners.
- **NEXT**: the canonical next action. **ACTOR**: root, or the running worker/specialist.
- **ROOT** / **ACTIVE**: the persistent session model and the model actually executing; they are shown separately only while a routed turn has swapped the model, otherwise one **MODEL** slot.
- **ROUTE**: who chose the active model — `direct`, `manual:<model>`, `<tier> via model-router`, `<tier> via model-router/H-MoE`, or `escalated→<model> via model-router`. Jev is never credited with a model choice.
- **S1**: System One's observed state — `off` (no plane), `ready` (bound, no evaluation yet), `eval` (a real evaluation in flight), `ok`, `degraded` (the last evaluation failed). A required System One failure is `BLOCKED` with `CONTROL S1` and `S1 degraded`; it is not ordinary `WORKING` progress.
- **COST**: the session's canonical current cost, with `(sub $…)` for spawned cost when present. **PROOF** and **CTX**: the projection's proof counters and context usage.

While an owner question is open the leading segment reads `NEEDS INPUT <question>` with `CONTROL USER` and a `BLOCK <question>` segment; the question itself stays in the normal question dialog:

```text
NEEDS INPUT choose onboarding behavior | CONTROL USER | ACTOR root | MODEL gpt-5.6 | ROUTE direct | S1 ok | COST $0.083 | BLOCK choose onboarding behavior
```

The row is two blocks: the phase text (`WORKING` / `NEEDS INPUT` / `BLOCKED` / `DONE` and its action) on the left, and every operator fact (`CONTROL` through `CTX`, `BLOCK` included) anchored to the right edge, so those columns never move while the left text changes length. The left text truncates first; only when the width cannot hold the right block beside sixteen cells of phase text do right segments drop in a fixed order — `CTX`, `PROOF`, the duplicate `ROOT`, a root `ACTOR`, `BLOCK`, `NEXT`, then a worker `ACTOR` — then the spawned-cost tail sheds and `ROUTE` compacts (`via router`); `WORKING`/`NEEDS INPUT`, `CONTROL`, `ACTIVE`/`MODEL`, `ROUTE`, `S1` and `COST` are never dropped, so control, routing and cost truth are not cut from the right. `S1 eval` is shown only while a real evaluation is in flight; a cancelled evaluation returns the label to its previous state and never reads `degraded`. The bar is render-only and never captures input; the editor stays interactive. Router configuration is described in `router-setup.md`.

The conversation viewport never emits the OSC 133 prompt-zone marks that the inline transcript components carry: a terminal may treat one as "a prompt starts here" and move the cursor to column 0, which corrupted framed rows in Herdr, and the alternate screen has no scrollback for those marks to navigate.

## Reading and copying

The workbench owns the mouse by default (`workbench.mouse: "on"`) and provides the two gestures a terminal would otherwise own: **drag inside the conversation to select, and the selection is copied when you release**; **right click pastes the system clipboard's text into the editor**. The wheel scrolls the surface under the pointer (conversation, Decision graph, inspector or Execution), header chips click, a click on the divider collapses or expands the work area, a drag on that divider, on the inspector/execution rule or on the graph/conversation rule resizes the panes, a click on a stage in the Decision graph opens its detail, and the title chips **Hide**, **Show plan**, **Maximize**, **Restore**, **List**, **Diagram**, **Hide** (graph) and the header's **Stacked** change the layout. `Alt+M` hands the mouse to the terminal instead (native selection and paste, no wheel scrolling in the workbench); the choice persists, the hint row reports the owner, and the change applies on the live screen. Most emulators also offer native selection while the workbench owns the mouse when Shift is held.

- `Alt+PageUp` / `Alt+PageDown`: scroll conversation without changing editor history. `Ctrl+End` or **Latest ↓** resumes following.
- `Ctrl+PageUp` / `Ctrl+PageDown`: page the Execution pane through the cycle's previews (`workbench.previews`, 24 by default, newest last); paging to the last page follows new evidence again. The complete record of every action stays in the transcript (`Ctrl+T`).
- Scrolling upward pauses following; the conversation header then reads *Reading* and offers **Latest ↓**.
- Wheel targets: inspector, execution or conversation under the pointer; title rows and gutters are not scroll targets, and an overflowing surface shows its visible row range in its title row. A plain click only focuses the pane; the selection starts when the pointer moves, and only then does the displayed text freeze while the model continues working. `Ctrl+X` copies the current selection again; tree-selector copying retains precedence.
- The conversation anchors to the bottom: the latest row sits directly above the status band, and a transcript shorter than the area leaves its empty rows above, never below.
- Reading position anchors to the entry under the top row. When live-history trimming removes that entry, the conversation resumes following instead of jumping to the oldest retained rows.
- A run whose own test check failed ends with its answer plus a warning line (`Verification remains unresolved …`); the inspector shows a **Checks** block listing every failing verification by command and directory. Later answers are ordinary answers; the obligations stay listed and keep `goal complete` from succeeding until the same check passes or you resolve them: `/verify` lists them, `/verify show <id>` details one, `/verify dismiss <id|all> [note]` resolves by your authority (recorded in the conversation; never counted as a passing test).
- **Copy conversation** or `Alt+C` copies user/assistant prose and answered `ask_question` interactions from the current session branch, including history outside the visible window. Routine tool output is excluded. Copies above 10 MiB are refused explicitly; use `/export` instead.
- `Alt+O`: collapse or expand the work area; `Alt+I`: hide or show the inspector; `Alt+X`: maximize or restore Execution; `Alt+=` / `Alt+-`: resize the work area; `Alt+G`: hide or show the Decision graph; `Alt+L`: cycle its view through List, Diagram and Lanes (one lane each for you, System One, root and workers); `Alt+P`: stacked or columns layout. With the mouse captured: the chips on the pane titles; drag the divider, the pane rules and the column gutter to resize. `Ctrl+T`: open the complete action transcript, including images and full results.
- Questions from the `ask_question` tool and System One clarifications open in the editor slot, under both columns, never over the graph or the work area. Up to three options ride one chip row with the active option's description beneath; more options, or a row the width cannot hold, become a numbered list. While a question is open the POV bar reads `NEEDS INPUT` with `CONTROL USER`, the Decider row says `waiting for you`, and the graph lights `YOU` with a waiting clock.

New shortcuts are configurable through the existing keybinding manager: `app.conversation.pageUp`, `app.conversation.pageDown`, `app.conversation.latest`, `app.conversation.copy`, `app.execution.toggle`, `app.inspector.toggle`, `app.execution.maximize`, `app.execution.pageUp`, `app.execution.pageDown`, `app.workbench.grow`, `app.workbench.shrink`, `app.workbench.layout`, `app.graph.toggle`, `app.graph.view`, and `app.mouse.toggle`. Editor shortcuts and active modal navigation retain their existing owners.

Terminal-native selection cannot notify the application to pause; the application-owned drag selection (mouse captured) does pause. Clipboard transport uses the existing platform clipboard/OSC 52 adapter; remote terminal support varies.

## Talking while it works

- Enter while the model is working queues the text as **steering**: it is delivered at the next model turn, after the running tool batch completes (`steeringMode: "all"` by default, so every queued steer goes together; `"one-at-a-time"` delivers one per turn and the live row says so). A `>>` prefix (or `Alt+Enter` where the terminal passes it; the hint row shows `>>` on Windows Terminal, which swallows the key) queues a **follow-up**, delivered after this run ends.
- The live row states the boundary: `Queued 1 · 1 steering → next model turn · enter send now · alt+up edit`.
- **Enter on an empty editor** while a message is queued interrupts the running turn and sends the queued steering and follow-up messages (including any queued during compaction) as one prompt — steering first, then follow-ups, images included. Queued extension commands are not messages and stay queued; the live row lists them separately and offers `enter send now` only when a message would go. The aborted turn's message reads `Operation aborted (send now)` in the session log, so a transcript can tell it from Escape. If the submission itself fails, the text returns to the editor and the failure is shown.
- `Escape` still interrupts and restores the queued text to the editor without sending it; `Alt+Up` recalls queued text for editing.
- During compaction the queue waits for compaction to end; send-now says so instead of interrupting it.

## File effects and limits

Edit diffs, written content, command results and failures appear above conversation. Routine reads stay in the action transcript. Explicit user shell commands remain visible in the upper band.

After foreground `bash` or `python` tools finish, a read-only Git observation can reveal silent file effects. It is an event-triggered workspace comparison, not a watcher, completion detector, attribution mechanism, or proof of a clean worktree. Unchanged pre-existing changes are excluded when a baseline is available. If execution overlaps the initial snapshot, the UI says that displayed changes may predate the task. Current patches may include prior or concurrent edits.

Observations are bounded to 128 dirty paths, 32 diff paths, 256 KiB per Git response, two seconds per Git command, and 64 KiB per small-file read. Larger files use metadata. Ignored files and changes outside the current working directory are not observed. Non-Git directories and budget failures produce a notice instead of a clean-workspace claim. File effects arriving after completion still update the folded receipt.

Conversation rendering visits visible entries lazily, retains at most 2 MiB of derived row-cache text, and uses existing terminal line-diff rendering. Frame cost is bounded by the visible rows, not by history: rows that already fit their zone skip the grapheme scan, so a 224×50 frame stays under a millisecond with 2,000 transcript entries where the previous full-transcript layout needed about sixteen. Execution retains up to `workbench.previews` bounded previews per cycle (24 by default, 4..200), shows them in order with the cycle's action and file-effect counts in its title row, and follows the newest rows until the operator scrolls up; a new preview follows again. No decorative animation timer or session-history scan runs per frame. Large individual visible messages still incur their renderer's normal cost.

## Verification

Confirmed by the [layout regressions](../test/workbench.test.ts), [input regressions](../test/workbench-controller.test.ts), [narrow-pane and native-editor regressions](../test/workbench-pane-regressions.test.ts), [headless terminal tests](../test/workbench-terminal.test.ts), the [Decision graph tests](../test/workbench-decision-graph.test.ts) (nine scenarios at 40–96 columns with exact row widths, one clock on the current node, the zone split with its rule and junctions, keys, chips, wheel, click and gutter drag, and headless resizes with the graph shown), the [stage log tests](../test/decision-stage-log.test.ts), the [evaluation ledger tests](../test/semantic-evaluation-ledger.test.ts) and the [POV bar tests](../test/operator-pov-bar.test.ts) (the right block's start column is identical across phase texts of different lengths): closed borders, stable geometry across growing output, independent pane scrolling, border-adjusted selection, cursor placement, resize and overlay focus restoration. Adjacent tests cover lazy rendering, cache bounds, questions, silent workspace changes, overlapping observations and stale-session fencing. These do not substitute for visual and clipboard acceptance in each user's terminal emulator.
